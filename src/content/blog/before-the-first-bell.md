---
title: Before the First Bell
date: 2026-08-02
excerpt: On trainings, conscious discipline, and the pressure of the school year to come—a reflection on meaning well, doing harm, and starting with simple sentences that give room to grow.
---

The start of the school year is coming. What a simple sentence to start with, but one wrapped up in pain and passion and pounds of pressure. Teaching middle schoolers feels more urgent than college-level students. Like every moment means more and holds more. Like an overflowing cup. That’s what it’s like to be a middle schooler, to be smaller than what the world makes you hold. I could even say that’s what it’s like to be human. The world won’t stop being bigger and scarier and our bodies stay the same through it all.

Brains break. The start of the school year is coming.

Starting again means trainings and orientation and going over all the things you could be doing wrong. We learn about conscious discipline. Furrow our thumbs into the ribcage of past lives and ask ourselves if we’re going to do to them what has been done to us. Verbally we all say ‘no’, but actions are harder to control. Am I a dysregulated adult overflowing into children who’ve spilled half themselves on the ground?

Verbally we all say ‘yes.’ Brains break.

I have never considered myself a mean person. What a simple sentence. I forget meanness can have less to do with intent and more to do with impact.

The start of the school year is coming and I can’t wait to build goodness into every action. To remind myself that spilling doesn’t mean starting over. To start with simple sentences that give room to grow.
